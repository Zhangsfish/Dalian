# CR-S01D｜Positioning Memo

## 1. Puzzle

The 2012 collapse of Bo Xilai's political position affected political networks linked to both Dalian and Chongqing. Yet the two local economies did not follow comparable post-2012 trajectories. Dalian experienced a pronounced slowdown in 2014–2015, whereas Chongqing retained relatively strong growth, investment and credit expansion.

The paper asks:

> **Why do political network shocks produce sharply different local economic outcomes?**

## 2. Core argument

The macroeconomic effect of a political-network shock is **state-dependent**.

A political shock is most consequential when it arrives in a local economy whose investment regime, industrial revenues and profits and credit system are already becoming fragile. Political connections may matter at the margin through project coordination, refinancing, risk assessment and credit access, but they do not substitute for the underlying profitability of the local growth model.

## 3. Empirical narrative

### Layer 1 — Regional slowdown
Dalian's growth gap relative to Wuhan and Qingdao deteriorates sharply after 2012, but only a smaller portion of this deterioration occurs relative to Liaoning. Real-estate and fixed-asset investment in Dalian and Liaoning collapse along highly similar paths.

### Layer 2 — Real-economy transmission
The aggregate GDP slowdown masks a much sharper deterioration in capital-intensive activity. Dalian's secondary-industry growth nearly stalls by 2015 while services continue to expand. Industrial revenues, profits and construction activity fall sharply.

### Layer 3 — Local financial amplification
As firm profitability and operating performance and project returns weaken, non-performing loans rise and bank credit growth falls increasingly below Liaoning's. Private investment also weakens. This is the most plausible location for an additional Dalian-specific amplification mechanism.

### Layer 4 — What did not disappear
Fiscal transfers, national policy platforms and major projects continue after 2012. The evidence therefore does not fit a simple story in which Dalian suddenly loses all access to higher-level resources.

### Layer 5 — Chongqing as a parallel exposed case
Chongqing experiences the same major political event but does not reproduce Dalian's investment and sharp credit slowdown. This comparison is central to the argument that political shocks interact with local structural conditions rather than mechanically determining macroeconomic outcomes.

## 4. Contribution

The article contributes to the literature on Chinese local political economy in three ways.

First, it shifts attention from whether political connections “matter” in the abstract to **when their loss has large macroeconomic consequences**.

Second, it separates visible public resources from marginal coordination and credit channels. A locality may continue to receive fiscal transfers and national projects while private and bank-financed activity deteriorates.

Third, it links political networks to the health of the local growth regime. Political connections appear more economically consequential when investment returns, firm profitability and operating performance and asset quality are already under pressure.

## 5. Why China Report

The paper is better suited to China Report than to a pure economics journal because its contribution is not a clean treatment-effect estimate. It is a structured political-economy case study using quantitative evidence to distinguish competing mechanisms.

It speaks directly to broader China-studies questions:
- local state capacity and political connections;
- regional economic restructuring;
- investment-led local growth;
- interaction between political institutions and financial conditions;
- heterogeneity of subnational development trajectories.

## 6. Claim discipline

The paper will make three levels of claim:

### Descriptive
Dalian slowed sharply relative to external comparison cities after 2012.

### Mechanistic
Most of the slowdown coincided with a broader Liaoning investment/industrial downturn, while Dalian displayed an additional credit and private-investment weakness.

### Interpretive
The loss or repricing of political-network value may have amplified adjustment through marginal credit and coordination channels, but the data do not support treating the full slowdown as a direct causal effect of the 2012 political event.

This hierarchy should remain stable throughout the English rewrite.
