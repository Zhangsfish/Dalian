## B. Reviewer 1｜China political economy

### B1. Why should a politician who left Dalian years earlier matter in 2012?
**Original risk: P1**

The historical link alone was insufficient.

Repair:
The 2013 court judgment documents that dealings involving Dalian International and Shide Group continued into Bo's later provincial and national posts, and that benefits from Shide chairman Xu Ming to Bo's family continued through 2012.

What this supports:
- Dalian-based relationships did not necessarily end when Bo left municipal office.

What it does **not** support:
- a claim that one measurable citywide patronage network remained intact through 2012.

This distinction is now explicit in Section 3.1.

### B2. Is the article becoming a Bo Xilai scandal narrative?
**PASS**

The title does not contain his name.
The court evidence is used narrowly to establish persistence of Dalian-based relationships, not to retell the criminal case.

### B3. What is the general contribution?
**PASS**

The paper's general claim is:
> the economic value of political networks is state-dependent and becomes more consequential when the local investment and financial regime is already vulnerable.

Dalian is the main case; Chongqing is used to test the mechanical version of the political story.

## C. Reviewer 2｜Quantitative methods / economics

### C1. Wuhan and Qingdao look hand-picked
**Risk: P1 → reduced**

The draft now states explicitly:
- they are complementary external benchmarks;
- they are **not** matched untreated controls;
- the result survives Wuhan-only and Qingdao-only comparisons;
- pre-event trend extrapolation is reported in the supplement.

Remaining limitation:
A two-city benchmark cannot reproduce a high-dimensional counterfactual.

Decision:
Acceptable for this journal if the paper does not present the −4.57 pp as a causal ATT.

### C2. Liaoning contains Dalian
**PASS**

The manuscript calls the Liaoning comparison a nested spatial decomposition, not an independent control experiment.

Interpretation retained:
- external divergence ≈ −4.57 pp;
- Dalian-vs-Liaoning deterioration ≈ −1.45 pp;
- the larger external divergence coincides with a broader Liaoning slowdown.

The paper does not interpret 1.45 / 4.57 as a causal share.

### C3. Liaoning data-quality problems
**Risk: P1 → repaired**

Officially acknowledged irregularities concerned fiscal-revenue data in some Liaoning cities/counties during 2011–2014.

The paper now states:
- this does not establish that the real-GDP growth series used here were fabricated;
- it strengthens the case for treating Liaoning as contextual evidence rather than a precise causal control;
- contemporaneous reported growth rates are used;
- statistically unusual 2016 observations remain outside the principal regional window.

### C4. “Cash flow” was not actually measured
**Original risk: P1 → repaired**

Available data measure:
- industrial revenue;
- industrial profit;
- industrial value added;
- construction output.

They do **not** directly measure firm cash flow.

Repairs:
- empirical headings and claims now use “industrial earnings”, “revenues and profits”, or “operating performance”;
- the manuscript explicitly states that revenue/profit are indicators of operating and earnings stress, not direct cash-flow measures.

### C5. “Credit contraction” overstates the data
**Original risk: P1 → repaired**

Dalian's loan balance continued to rise in 2013–2015; what collapsed was the **growth rate**, not the stock of credit.

Repairs:
- “credit contraction” changed to “credit weakening” / “sharp credit slowdown” / “slower bank-credit growth”;
- keyword changed from “credit contraction” to “bank credit”.

### C6. Supply or demand?
**Original risk: P1 → repaired**

Aggregate loan data cannot separate:
- tighter bank-credit supply;
- weaker borrowing demand caused by poorer project returns.

The manuscript now states this directly.

Therefore the claim is:
> bank-credit conditions and growth weakened,

not:
> a pure credit-supply shock has been identified.

### C7. Mechanism or mediation?
**PASS**

The paper does not regress GDP on post-treatment mechanism variables and call coefficient shrinkage mediation.

The mechanism evidence is presented as:
- temporal;
- spatial;
- cross-outcome consistency.

## D. Reviewer 3｜Regional / China studies

### D1. What does “regional vulnerability” actually mean?
**Strengthened**

The paper now grounds the concept in:
- the 2014–2015 Dalian/Liaoning real-estate and FAI downturn;
- the collapse in secondary-industry performance;
- industrial revenue and profit deterioration;
- worsening NPLs and slower bank credit.

Additional temporal evidence added:
- Dalian government-fund revenue −56.3% in 2012;
- Dalian land-sale revenue −45.4%;
- Liaoning government-fund revenue −28.7%.

These indicators show that land/property stress was already visible in the transition year before the sharp 2014 GDP divergence.

### D2. Is Japan a rival explanation?
**PASS**

Japanese new-project entry fell substantially, but 2013–2014 actual Japanese investment remained high.

Interpretation:
- useful as a slow-moving structural headwind;
- weak as a discrete explanation of the 2014 break.

### D3. Did higher-level resources disappear?
**PASS WITH NARROW CLAIM**

Only 2014–2015 fiscal categories are cleanly comparable.

The paper now explicitly says:
- those data cannot establish that support was unchanged from pre-2012 levels;
- they only show no collapse in the observed budgetary channel during the key slowdown.

National platforms and large projects provide additional evidence of formal-resource continuity.

## E. Chongqing audit

### Original issue
Several draft phrases made Chongqing sound like it received “the same treatment”.

That is too strong:
- Bo was the incumbent party secretary in Chongqing in 2012;
- his relationship to Dalian was historical;
- administrative status and growth regimes differ.

### Repair
The article now calls Chongqing a **parallel exposed case** and explicitly states:

> This is not a same-treatment test.

Its role is narrower:
- to challenge the general mechanical proposition that one elite political rupture is sufficient by itself to cause a local investment collapse and sharp credit slowdown.

This is a heterogeneity / conditionality comparison, not a clean control.
