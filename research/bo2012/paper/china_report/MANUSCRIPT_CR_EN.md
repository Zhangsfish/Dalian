# Political Network Shocks and Local Economic Vulnerability: Evidence from Dalian after 2012

## Abstract

**Purpose:** This article examines why the 2012 collapse of a major political network was followed by a sharp economic slowdown in Dalian but not by a comparable downturn in Chongqing.

**Approach:** It combines city-level growth comparisons, a nested Dalian–Liaoning comparison, sectoral and financial data, evidence on fiscal and project resources, and Chongqing as a parallel exposed case.

**Findings:** Dalian’s growth gap relative to Wuhan and Qingdao deteriorated by about 4.6 percentage points between 2008–2011 and 2013–2015, while its deterioration relative to Liaoning was only about 1.5 points. The regional slowdown coincided with collapsing real-estate and fixed-asset investment. Within Dalian, secondary-industry growth, industrial revenues and profits weakened sharply, non-performing loans rose, and bank credit slowed, even as fiscal transfers, national policy platforms and large projects continued. Chongqing did not display a similar investment or credit collapse after the same political shock.

**Conclusion:** Political network shocks do not translate mechanically into local economic decline. Their macroeconomic consequences depend on the structural vulnerability of local investment regimes, corporate cash flow and credit systems.

**Keywords:** political networks; local economic vulnerability; Dalian; Liaoning; credit contraction; local political economy
